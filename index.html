Calm Your Mind

Use these evidence-based techniques when you feel panic, high anxiety, or heavy emotional distress.

4-7-8 Relaxation Breathing

Inhale for 4 seconds, hold your breath for 7 seconds, and exhale slowly for 8 seconds to lower heart rate and reduce stress.

5-4-3-2-1 Grounding Technique

Focus on your immediate physical senses to return to the present moment when feeling overwhelmed.

5

LOOK: Notice 5 things around you right now (a lamp, a window, your hand).

4

TOUCH: Feel 4 textures near you (your clothes, chair surface, cool desk).

3

LISTEN: Identify 3 distinct sounds in your environment (traffic, fan, birds).

2

SMELL: Notice 2 scents around you (coffee, soap, fresh air).

1

TASTE: Focus on 1 taste in your mouth or sip a glass of water.

How to Help Someone in Need

Recognizing warning signs and knowing how to respond can save a life.

1

Recognize Warning Signs

  • Talking about wanting to die or feeling hopeless.
  • Withdrawing from family, friends, or social activities.
  • Giving away prized possessions or saying goodbyes.
  • Extreme mood swings or sudden unnatural calm.
2

Listen Without Judgment

  • Give them your undivided attention.
  • Avoid saying "things could be worse" or giving quick fixes.
  • Acknowledge their pain gently: "I am here for you."
  • Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?"
3

Connect to Professional Help

  • Encourage them to call a helpline like Tele-MANAS (14416).
  • Offer to stay with them while they call.
  • Do not leave them unattended if they are in immediate danger.
  • Remove access to self-harm means if safe to do so.
Breaking Stigma

Myths vs. Facts About Suicide

Understanding the reality helps us create a supportive environment without stigma.

Myth

"Asking someone if they are thinking about suicide will give them the idea."

Fact

Asking directly reduces anxiety, opens up honest communication, and shows that you care and are willing to support them.

Myth

"People who talk about suicide are just seeking attention."

Fact

Talking about suicide is often a cry for help. Always take statements regarding suicide seriously and offer support.

Myth

"Only people with severe mental illness attempt suicide."

Fact

Many people who attempt suicide experience deep emotional pain, overwhelm, or acute crisis without having a diagnosed mental illness.
